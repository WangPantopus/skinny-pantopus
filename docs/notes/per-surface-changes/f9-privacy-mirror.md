- (research contradiction, topic trust-provenance-privacy: where facts co) v1: Caption: 'Your post lands somewhere inside this circle. It is re-rolled every time you post.' Ring about 150 m radius, scale bar '500 ft'. | research: The shipped transform (backend/utils/locationPrivacy.js, approx_area) is a keyed HMAC offset of ±0.005° in lat and lng, 'stable per entity so pins do not flicker', rounded to 0.001°. At 45.6°N that is a box of about ±556 m × ±389 m, so roughly 92% of possible public points fall outside a 150 m circle. The caption is false twice over: the offset is not re-rolled, and the area is about 12× larger than drawn. Andrés et al. (Geo-Indistinguishability) show that independent noise per report degrades privacy linearly with the number of reports, so the stable offset is the better design and the copy describes the worse one. | do: Rewrite as: 'Neighbors see your posts at this spot, the same spot every time, about 0.3 mi from your address.' Take the drawn geometry from the server parameters: either the single public point, or the true ±0.005° box with a scale bar in tenths of a mile. Add a contract test that fails if the mirror's radius or box constant differs from locationPrivacy.js.
- (research contradiction, topic trust-provenance-privacy: where facts co) v1: Draw a jitter ring centred on the user's location, with no pin, no house marker and no dot at the centre, because 'the centre is the thing being hidden'. | research: The audience-view research (Lipford 2008: accuracy 66%→94%) and Facebook's 'View As' show the audience's literal view, not a model of the mechanism. What a neighbor literally sees on the Nearby map is one jittered pin. A ring drawn centred on the home still encodes the home's position as its geometric centre, so a shared screenshot gives away the very point the design is meant to hide. | do: Draw the public pin exactly where neighbors see it, labelled 'What neighbors see', with no home marker and no region centred on the home. Add a caption giving the distance from the address, and an explicit row 'Hidden from neighbors: your street address'. If a range must be shown, don't centre it on the home in any shareable frame.
- (research contradiction, topic Competitive and adjacent product teardow) v1: The caption says 'Your post lands somewhere inside this circle. It is re-rolled every time you post.' presented as the protection, next to 'Your public posts: 7'. | research: Gizmodo (2019) mapped up to 20,000 Ring camera locations by aggregating about 65,800 Neighbors posts whose coordinates were hidden from the UI. Aggregation across posts is the documented threat. The claim that per-post random jitter centred on one point shrinks under averaging (roughly the radius divided by the square root of the post count) is reasoning here, not a fetched source. | do: Have a privacy engineer confirm how the jitter is actually generated before this copy ships. Prefer a stable coarse cell (one fixed offset or a snapped centroid per place) over a fresh random offset per post. Change the caption to describe what cannot be recovered even across many posts, and don't present re-rolling as a protection.
