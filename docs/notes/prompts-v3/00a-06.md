ACCESSIBILITY (print)
- The word carries the state and is read at the end of the row label: "Comcast, 79 dollars 99, due in 14 days, Monday 2 November, Upcoming". The glyph is decorative (hidden from assistive tech).
- Non-colour cue: the word. Target: never the chip; the row is (B13).

COPY (print)
- Exactly one chip per row. Sentence case. Dates show the weekday.
- Beyond tomorrow, deadlines, due dates and expiries lead with the relative count: "in 7 days · Mon 26 Oct"; "Expires in 7 days · Mon 26 Oct"; "due in 4 days · Fri 23 Oct". Tonight, today and tomorrow stand alone ("Today"), and a date or time today shows no weekday (write "today").
- Past dates on chips show the date only, with no "x days ago": "Declined · Thu 15 Oct". This is an exception to B16 (06.A5).
- Never "Paid" as a verb in notifications; write "marked paid".

PLATFORMS (print): no platform differences.

INSTEAD OF (print)
- Warning-hue text on a warning tint → text.strong on surface.sunken with the hue on the glyph only, because warning text on its tint is about 3:1.
- Two chips on one row → the one status that matters, because two chips compete and get misread.
- A status chip on a person ("Verified", "Active") → the role in plain text, because people are not statuses.
- A warning-tinted countdown pill (founding window) → a neutral date chip, because countdowns pressure people.
- The date before the count ("Mon 26 Oct · in 7 days") → "in 7 days · Mon 26 Oct", because the house style and DateRow lead with the count and every consuming surface copies this chip.
- A disc-shaped status dot → the listed glyphs, because circles belong to ProvenanceMark.
