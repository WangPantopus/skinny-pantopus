## Why the newly identified units are different

The City’s public quarterly assessment service provides a separate real-estate account and condo interior row for a converted unit. The FY2026 Q3 real-estate export and master-parcel table each had one parent row for every one of the 19,573 properties, no named child parcel under those parents and no unit number on those rows. Each parent had exactly one residential attribute line. In FY2027 Q1, 39 parents became condo mains, 19,533 continued with one residential line and one switched to another class. The analysis traced the two city parent/child keys, individual condo use, unit designator, living area and its own bedrooms, full baths and half baths. All 92 exact candidates have positive bedroom and living-area counts. Do not ingest first-quarter estimated tax dollars as a certified new valuation. The City explains that qualifying 2025 condo recordings appear on FY2027 first-quarter bills. [City quarterly assessment service](https://gisportal.boston.gov/arcgis/rest/services/Assessing/properties_boston_gov/FeatureServer) · [City conversion timeline](https://content.boston.gov/departments/assessing/condo-conversions-and-tax-bills) · [City assessing calendar](https://www.boston.gov/departments/assessing/assessing-calendar)

The five other numeric rows disagreed on street, street number or city. The six remaining child rows failed the initial original-unit and genuine condo screen. A city condominium account or a shared street address by itself does not establish that it is the same original apartment. Exclude accessory condo storage and parking. A distinct assessed child is needed even when several apartments have the same design. The remaining ordinary apartments still lack an authoritative interior record for each individual unit.

## Source rights and protected records

The founder previously confirmed household display of the covered City assessment fields after reviewing Boston’s download terms. The City’s official open-data policy describes the default CC BY 4.0 license for City Open Data; its general download agreement also contains restrictions on third-party redistribution. Preserve the exact source and applicable terms with every release, attribute the City and avoid publicly redistributing its raw files.
