# Other routes: reaching people that free county files don't

November 7, 2026. Research for decision D2 ([decisions.md](decisions.md)); nothing here is built, bought or sent. It
covers every legitimate route besides counties' free files and one-home lookups: state rules that make an agency hand
over a copy, state tax departments, GIS clearinghouses, federal data sets, what changed for the 41 counties in
[public-records-requests.md](public-records-requests.md), and what would raise bedrooms and baths, the weakest facts.
Statewide files have their own document: [statewide-inventory.md](statewide-inventory.md). Lookup sites:
[lookup-sites.md](lookup-sites.md). Counts only; no addresses or owner names.

This isn't legal advice. Prices and terms are as read on the date shown.

## The answer

- **The biggest routes need no one's permission:** Texas's built statewide file (the founder's publish command, up to
  12.25M people), New York's tax department roll (3.30M people in 23 counties, plus market value upstate), and the other
  free statewide files in [statewide-inventory.md](statewide-inventory.md) (49.1M people in all with Texas).
- **State tax departments are the second-best route.** Tennessee's Comptroller (3.76M people, free, by a person's form),
  Indiana's DLGF (6.92M, free files plus building facts by email), Montana's DOR (1.14M, free, adds bedrooms and baths to
  what's stored), Kansas's Property Valuation Division (up to 2.97M, $250 a county) and New York's (above).
- **Bedrooms and baths:** the largest single lift is Florida's 62 property appraisers outside the five already
  connected: 15.94M people. The state roll in storage has none; appraisers' own files usually do (the five stored ones
  do), but these 62 weren't opened here, and most need a person's download or a purchase. Next: Montana's free files
  (1.14M), Clark County NV's one-month purchase (2.40M, $300), and written OKs from King WA (2.34M) and Fairfax VA (1.16M).
  Household entries (WP9) are the only route that reaches every home.
- **Of the 41 counties, 8 changed:** Suffolk NY and San Bernardino are now stored; Nassau gets value and lot free from New
  York's roll; the rest of Milwaukee and Dane counties get value and lot from Wisconsin's file; Spokane's value and address
  sit in Washington's statewide file; St. Louis County's own layer has year built, size and value; Suffolk MA is stored.
  Building facts for the other 33 still need the founder's letters or purchases.
- **Federal data fills no home's record.** HUD's files give a building's bedroom mix for assisted housing (about 200,000
  buildings and properties), not a unit's; the Census and FEMA sets stay area figures and labeled estimates, as WP7
  already does.

## 1. State rules that make an agency hand over a copy

Every state's records law reaches electronic records, and most let a requester choose an electronic copy of a database
the agency already keeps, at copying cost. What differs is who may ask, what a commercial requester pays, and a few
rules aimed at property data. The full state-by-state table, with the statute and leading case for each, is in
[lookup-sites.md](lookup-sites.md#state-law-a-non-lawyers-reading); the points that matter for a bulk copy:

- **The requester picks an electronic copy** in most states, by statute or court ruling: for example Arkansas (Ark. Code
  25-19-105(d)), California (Gov. Code 7922.570), Colorado (C.R.S. 24-72-205), Connecticut (C.G.S. 1-211), Georgia
  (O.C.G.A. 50-18-71(f): the format the agency keeps, or a standard export format), Maryland (GP
  4-205(c)), Mississippi (25-61-10(2)), Nebraska (84-712(3)), Nevada (NRS 239.010(4)), North Carolina (G.S. 132-6.2), Ohio
  (R.C. 149.43(B)(6)), Oklahoma (native format, an appeals court ruling of September 2024), Oregon (ORS 192.324(3)),
  Vermont (1 V.S.A. 316(i)), West Virginia (29B-1-3(3)), Wyoming (16-4-202(d)(ii)). Source: the
  [Reporters Committee's Open Government Guide](https://www.rcfp.org/open-government-guide/), each state's section I.C.5.
- **Courts have ordered property data handed over in bulk:** New Hampshire ordered a city to copy its computer tape of
  property tax cards (*Menge v. City of Manchester*, 113 N.H. 533 (1973)); West Virginia rejected an assessor's attempt
  to refuse CAMA files to an out-of-state commercial vendor (*Hurlbert v. Matkovich*, 233 W. Va. 583 (2014)); Virginia
  held a commercial tax service may use the Act (*Associated Tax Service v. Fitzpatrick*, 236 Va. 181 (1988)); Louisiana's
  Attorney General said a requester's plan to sell tax rolls doesn't change their public status (Op. 90-330), and that
  computer records of sales and assessment rolls are public (Op. 87-301); Michigan held tax data held by a contractor is
  still a public record (*MacKenzie v. Wales Twp.*, 247 Mich. App. 124 (2001)); California held a county can't attach
  license terms to its GIS base map under the Public Records Act (*County of Santa Clara v. Superior Court*, 170 Cal.
  App. 4th 1301 (2009)).
- **But format can be the agency's:** Louisiana's appeals court held an assessor need only produce records in the format
  it keeps them, in a case brought by a listing site (*Zillow, Inc. v. Bealer*, 333 So.3d 854 (La. App.)), and
  Alabama's Attorney General said a state agency needn't produce electronically what it has on paper.
- **Rules aimed at property data:** Pennsylvania lets agencies price "integrated property assessment lists" and GIS at
  market value (65 P.S. 67.1307(b)(4)); North Carolina lets an agency make a GIS requester agree not to resell (G.S.
  132-10); California makes property characteristics available for a fee reflecting actual cost (R&T 408.3), so prices
  can be high; South Carolina's Supreme Court let a county copyright "specially-created" GIS data and limit commercial use
  (*Seago v. Horry County*, 663 S.E.2d 38 (S.C. 2008)); New York's appeals court let a town withhold its inventory data
  from a commercial requester on privacy grounds (*Comps, Inc. v. Town of Islip*, 2006, cited in
  [public-records-requests.md](public-records-requests.md)).
- **Residency limits:** Alabama, Arkansas, Delaware, New Jersey, Tennessee and Virginia limit records requests to their own
  residents or citizens, and Kentucky's nonresidents have no enforceable right; the Supreme Court upheld Virginia's limit
  (*McBurney v. Young*, 569 U.S. 221 (2013)). A request there needs an in-state requester or the county's own sale route.
- **Commercial steps:** Arizona (state the commercial purpose, A.R.S. 39-121.03), Illinois (disclose it, 5 ILCS 140/3.1),
  Kentucky (a higher fee, KRS 61.874(4)), Oklahoma (a higher fee, 51 O.S. 24A.5(3)), and lists of names: Washington (RCW
  42.56.070(9)), Kansas (K.S.A. 45-220(c)(2)), Idaho (Code 74-120), Montana (MCA 2-6-1017). Pantopus asks for building
  facts without owner names, which keeps it outside the list rules.

## 3. GIS clearinghouses and regional agencies

- **PASDA** (Penn State, [pasda.psu.edu](https://www.pasda.psu.edu/)): county parcel layers for some of Pennsylvania's 67
  counties, mostly boundaries and IDs. Pennsylvania has no statewide file, and its law lets agencies price assessment
  data at market value.
- **Kentucky DGI** ([hub](https://www.arcgis.com/home/search?q=owner%3AKentucky_DGI%20parcel)): parcels for some counties,
  without assessment facts in the one opened (Webster).
- **Southern California Association of Governments** ([2019 annual land use](https://www.arcgis.com/home/item.html?id=888e4b1f8d0447b5af805a9d80559092)):
  5,131,098 parcels in six counties with APN, land use and building footprint area; planning use only, no building facts.
  It gives Orange and Ventura a parcel's use, nothing more.
- **Bay Area Metro's data portal** ([data.bayareametro.gov](https://data.bayareametro.gov/)): county uploads, including
  Sonoma's public parcel layer (188,969 parcels) and Solano's parcels with year built and areas. Leads for WP10, not the
  41 counties.
- **St. Louis County GIS** ([STLCO_STC_Parcels_Revised](https://www.arcgis.com/home/item.html?id=06d43012de4a484ab004f5b34ea0e8e8)):
  the county's own layer credited to its Assessor: 400,796 parcels, every one with an address and acres, year built on
  370,529, residential size on 356,305, appraised value on 393,931, tax year 2023. Disclaimer and hold-harmless only. A
  planning layer, so ask whether a current one is kept.
- The state clearinghouses that hold statewide files (Colorado OIT, Wisconsin SCO, VGIN, NDGISHub, VCGI, GRANIT, AGIO) are
  in [statewide-inventory.md](statewide-inventory.md).

## 6. Bedrooms and baths: what would raise them most

Storage today: 61.1M people live in counties where at least half of stored homes carry both bedrooms and baths (coverage
report, November 7). The routes, ranked by people they could add:

1. **Household entries (WP9):** every verified household, the only route that reaches every home. Built, off.
2. **Florida's 62 other appraisers: 15.94M people.** The state roll in storage has no bedrooms or baths; the five
   appraisers' files already connected do (Miami-Dade, Broward, Hillsborough, Sarasota, Seminole), and WP10 found the
   others' files would add them, without opening each. Orange (1.53M), Pinellas (0.97M), Lee (0.86M), Polk (0.85M),
   Brevard, Pasco, Volusia, Manatee, Collier and others publish downloads, several refusing scripts; Palm Beach and
   Duval sell theirs. A person's download per county, then WP10 connectors.
3. **Kansas CAMA through the state: up to 2.97M**, if the residential fields hold bedrooms and baths (not confirmed);
   $250 a county.
4. **Clark County NV: 2.40M**, $300 for a month of the data site (fee schedule July 2024).
5. **King County WA: 2.34M**, a written OK for the weekly residential file.
6. **Tennessee: up to 3.76M**, if the Assessment_Data fields include them (the dictionary decides).
7. **Montana: 1.14M**, free, already stored without them; fill unknown (optional entry in the state's CAMA).
8. **Fairfax VA: 1.16M**, a written OK.
9. **Virginia's local tables: 0.61M** with both (0.73M with bedrooms), free now.
10. **Smaller asks:** Polk IA (0.52M), Washoe NV (0.51M), Lee FL's own file (covered in 2), Greenville SC ($500,
    0.57M), Johnson KS ($100 a year, 0.63M), San Mateo ($305) and Ventura (about $465).

Where none of these reaches, the honest answer stays a one-tap question and a labeled estimate, as decided.

## 7. Left out on purpose

D2 keeps resellers, listing sites and national licenses out. For the record, if the founder ever reconsiders: reseller
copies of a county's file run about $200-300 a county; a national bulk property license starts around $80,000 a year;
a metered property API about $202 a month for 5,100 calls; MLS access about $1,000 to set up plus $1,200 a quarter per MLS
([cost-and-rights.md](cost-and-rights.md#3-prices-found-for-decisions-only)).
