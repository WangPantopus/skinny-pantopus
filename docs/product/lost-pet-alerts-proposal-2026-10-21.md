## The question

On October 21 the founder asked which one thing to add to make the app cool and bring many people to download and use it.

## The answer

Lost pet alerts: one tap turns a missing dog or cat into a search across the neighborhood.

1. The owner adds a photo, the pet's name and kind, and where and when it was last seen.
2. People whose home or saved place is within about a mile get one push, held during quiet hours.
3. Pantopus makes a print-ready flyer with a QR code, and one link for Facebook groups, Nextdoor and texts. Every channel opens the same pet page.
4. Anyone can tap "I saw them" on that page and drop a pin, with no app and no account. The household sees each sighting on a map.
5. Photos of found pets nearby are compared with the missing pet: "Is this Biscuit?"
6. The owner marks the pet home, and everyone who helped hears it.

## Why this one

- It's the one moment people want every stranger within a mile to hear from them. Each flyer puts a Pantopus QR code on poles in the right streets, and each post on another site links back to a Pantopus page.
- It helps the owner with nobody nearby on Pantopus. The flyer, the link and the sightings page work on day one; the push gets better as neighbors join.
- Strangers meet Pantopus by helping, without signing up, and the page offers the reason to install: "Get alerts for lost pets near you."
- It fits the rules the feed already keeps: no crime or suspicion heads-ups. This is the cooperative opposite.

## Limits

- **It makes no money as designed.** It's a way to bring people in. Ways it could earn later, each its own decision: paid boosts to reach more people (some lost-pet services sell these), sponsorship by local vets and pet stores, and QR pet tags. Charging people while their pet is missing needs care.
- It isn't a daily habit. A neighborhood may see a few alerts a month; Today, Place and Plans have to keep people.
- Lost-pet services already exist (PawBoost, Petco Love Lost, Facebook groups, Nextdoor). The difference is one tap to all of it, and a page that works for people who never install anything.
- On October 3 the advice was to add no features until one stranger signs up for one reason and comes back. This was the one exception, because its job is to produce that stranger.

## Not chosen

- A Halloween treat map: Halloween 2026 falls on a Saturday, but a mostly empty map in launch week looks dead.
- More Support Trains: the one feature people already use, but its helpers are friends across town, not neighbors.
- Leaning on Plans: the coolest thing in the app, but joining a stranger's plan needs neighbors on the app first.
